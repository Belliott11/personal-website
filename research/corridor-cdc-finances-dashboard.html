The Balance Sheet of Institutional Memory

A companion piece to Tracing Institutional Memory, a STAR research project that found the median completed tenure of a Philadelphia CDC executive director is just two years. That project was built on interviews and hand-verified leadership histories; this one asks what the money says. Built on Form 990 filings for 103 Philadelphia community development corporations.

Where Institutional Memory Is Under Strain

An instability score built from two signals in each organization's filing history: years with an abrupt shift in officer compensation (often a leadership change happening off the record) and years with no 990 on file at all. Higher means more turbulence. Yorktown CDC's score of 13 is more than double the next-highest organization's.

04

The Ones That Went Dark — Verified

Six organizations whose filing history — a stopped 990, a multi-year gap, or both — flagged them as likely inactive. Rather than publish that inference as fact, each one was checked by hand against ProPublica's live organization records and public reporting. Only half held up. Two are demonstrably still operating (one filed a 990 as recently as this year), and a third is a small, still-active civic association that simply doesn't file full financial paperwork. That's the honest result of this method: a stale-filing signal is a lead worth checking, not proof of closure.

05

Does the City's Tax Credit Help Orgs Survive?

Philadelphia's CDC Tax Credit program subsidizes participating organizations' operating costs. The honest answer, in this sample, is: not obviously. Recently-filing rates are nearly identical whether or not an org is in the program — institutional survival looks like it depends on other things.

Data & methods

  1. Source. This analysis runs on Corridor, a Philadelphia CDC dataset built in collaboration with Adam Mirmina (corridor-phl.vercel.app). It catalogues 103 Philadelphia community development corporations from three sources: a hand-compiled research roster (70 orgs, with follow-up research into founding years and leadership history), Temple University Special Collections Research Center finding aids (29 orgs), and the City of Philadelphia's own CDC Tax Credit program participant list.
  2. Financial and current-leadership data (this page's entire basis) comes from IRS Form 990 filings via the ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer API, fuzzy-matched to the roster and confidence-scored rather than guessed. 760 organization-year financial records, 2008–2024 (2024 excluded from charts here — only 2 orgs had filed by the time this data was pulled).
  3. The honest limitation: ProPublica's officer/board data reflects each organization's latest 990, not a full year-by-year history — there is no free, lightweight way to pull historical Form 990 Part VII names at scale (the AWS 990 mirror was retired; IRS per-file XML URLs 404; ProPublica blocks scripted PDF downloads). The "instability score" here is a financial-filing proxy, not a confirmed leadership-change count. Where a fuller, name-level leadership history exists, it comes from direct research by hand — that's the companion qualitative project, Tracing Institutional Memory, not this one.
  4. Closure candidates are inferred from filing gaps and stale "last year filed" dates, not confirmed dissolution filings — and that inference is only half-reliable. Of the 6 organizations the Signals sheet flagged, direct verification against ProPublica's live organization pages and public reporting confirmed 3 as genuinely closed, found 2 to be filing and operating normally as recently as fiscal year 2025, and found 1 (HMC Squared Community Association) to be a small, still-active civic association that simply doesn't file full financial paperwork. Section 04 reports only the verified result, not the raw flag. One confirmed-closed organization shows negative reported revenue in its final year on file — most likely a reporting anomaly in the source 990, kept here rather than silently corrected.
  5. Tax credit comparison compares 2023-or-later filing rates between the 28 roster organizations flagged as tax-credit-active and the 42 that aren't. Sample sizes are small and this is an observational comparison, not a causal one.
